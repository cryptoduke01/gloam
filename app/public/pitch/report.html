01The problem

Every stablecoin payment is public.

On a public chain anyone can see who paid whom, how much, and when. Pay your team in USDC and every salary sits on the explorer. Pay a supplier and competitors read your costs. Run an AI agent and its spending shows what it buys.

The people building stablecoin payments say the same thing. Paradigm's Matt Huang calls public payments "a non-starter for many businesses". Tempo's own launch post says payroll on a public ledger would publish every salary. JPMorgan lists privacy beside compliance and finality as what institutions need.

02Why now

The money moved on chain this year. Privacy did not come with it.

The rules now favour privacy you can prove

  • 5 Oct 2026: FinCEN withdrew its proposed rule against mixers.
  • Dec 2025: the SEC's privacy roundtable backed selective disclosure and zero-knowledge proofs.
  • 2025: courts struck down the Tornado Cash sanctions, and the DOJ said it will not charge developers for what users do.
  • What still applies: stablecoin issuers must be able to freeze. Gloam's design never stops them.
03The idea

A payment has one owner. So nobody else needs a key.

Privacy in crypto splits into two different jobs, and they need different tools.

Shared secrets

FHE and MPC

Many people's hidden data has to be combined: a sealed-bid auction, a private order book, a lending pool. Someone has to compute on it, so a group of servers holds the key.

Zama, Arcium

One owner

Zero-knowledge proofs on your device

Your money is yours alone. You prove the payment on your own device and the chain only checks the proof. No group, server or operator ever holds a key.

Gloam: payments, payroll, proof of funds

Payments are the biggest one-owner job in finance. That is where Gloam lives.

We do not invent new cryptography, and for payments we should not. Gloam uses the zero-knowledge maths Zcash has run on for years and builds the product on top, the way Stripe built payments people wanted on top of the card networks. Zama and Arcium spent years and over $100M building encryption engines for shared secrets. A payment does not need one.

The thesis

Privacy for payments should belong to the person who owns the money, not to a network that holds the keys.

When a committee or operator holds the keys, one breach can expose everyone, including every past payment. When the owner holds them, a breach exposes one person. That is the property finance needs, and it is the one Gloam is built on.

04How it works

Two ways to make a payment private.

Committee modelZama, Arcium, Tempo Zones

You send data→ The network computes on it→ A committee or operator holds the key

GloamClient-side proofs

Your device makes a proof→ The chain checks it→ Nobody else holds a key

Money goes into a shared private balance on the chain. Inside it, payments move as sealed notes that only the owner can open. A private payment proves in under a second on a laptop and runs on normal contracts on Tempo and Robinhood Chain. There is no admin withdraw, and any rule change waits three days in public.

vs Zama

FHE, $170M+ raised
What they are
An encryption company. Tokens stay encrypted and a coprocessor network computes on them.
Our edge
No key committee. Zama's 13 key holders share one global key, so a breach could unlock every balance ever encrypted. Gloam's keys never leave the owner, and a payment proves in under a second instead of running heavy encrypted maths.
Where they win
Computing on data many people share, such as sealed auctions and lending pools.

vs Arcium

MPC, Solana
What they are
A network of nodes that compute together on secret pieces of data, on Solana.
Our edge
No node cluster to trust or pay for each computation, and no lock-in to one chain. Gloam runs on plain contracts on any EVM chain, starting with the two built for payments.
Where they win
Private shared state on Solana, such as dark pools and private matching.

vs Tempo Zones

Tempo's own privacy
What they are
Private side chains on Tempo, each run by an operator.
Our edge
Zones hide payments from the public, but the operator sees everything and deposits and withdrawals stay public. With Gloam nobody but the owner sees inside, and the owner chooses what to prove.
Where they win
Enterprises that want an operator in the loop, built into Tempo itself.

06Is this new?

Not new cryptography. A new building block for finance.

Judges will ask whether this has been done before. Here is the exact answer.

Not new, and we say so

  • The private pool. The design goes back to Zcash in 2016. Tornado, Railgun and Cloak use it too.
  • Showing part of your money. Zcash and Railgun have viewing keys, Privacy Pools proves where money came from, and Zama grants decryption rights.
  • The proof system. Groth16 proofs written in Circom, the same proven tools much of the industry uses.

New, as far as we found

  • Payroll total proof. A company proves a run of up to 32 payments adds up to exactly the total, across a set number of people, paid from its own money, without showing who got what. We found nothing like it.
  • Proofs sealed to one reader and a date. Who the proof is for, when it expires, and which chain and pool it covers are sealed into the proof. Change the label and the proof fails. Anyone checks it in a browser with no wallet.
  • Private payments for AI agents. Tempo and Stripe's machine payments spec had no private method; ours is the one proposed. Gloam also seals x402 payments and gives agents handles to money instead of secrets.

In crypto, a primitive is a building block other people build on. That is how Gloam ships these pieces: the payroll total proof and sealed proofs are open to any wallet, payroll tool or agent through the SDK, the API and the MCP server, so nobody has to build their own.

Honest limit: whoever receives a proof can still pass it on. Locking a proof so only one named reader can check it is the next step.

Gloam is not a new cipher. It is a new building block for finance: private payments you can prove.

07What we built

Proofs you can show to one person, for a set time.

Hiding money is not enough for a business. An auditor, a lender or a client needs to see something. Gloam lets the owner prove one fact and nothing else.

Other private systems hand over a viewing key, which shows your whole history forever. A Gloam proof names who it is for, expires, and can be checked by anyone in a browser with no wallet.

10Business model

Three ways Gloam makes money.

Transaction fees

0.1% on deposits and 0.1% on cash outs, from mainnet. Railgun charges 0.25% each way.

Payroll runs

A fee per payroll run with the auditor proof included, then team plans for approvals and accountant proofs.

API usage

Partner API and SDK usage, including proof checks for apps and agents.

People already pay for privacy. Railgun charges 0.25% in and 0.25% out of its pool and earned about $4.3M in fees over the last twelve months. Because Tempo and Arc may bundle basic privacy for free, Gloam prices what they do not offer: payroll, proofs and agent payments.

12Why this pitch wins

Privacy wins when it is pitched as money.

Across five Colosseum hackathons, privacy projects took 6 of 180 placed awards. Payments and stablecoin projects took 30.

Every privacy project that placed opened with a user's problem and won through a money track. Projects that led with the technology stopped at honorable mention. So Gloam leads with private stablecoin payments for people, teams and agents, and uses the trust model as the reason a committee design cannot copy it.

13Risks and answers

What a tough judge will ask, and what we say.

Risk

The setup ceremony had one participant, so in theory that person could forge proofs.

Answer

A public multi-party ceremony, like Zcash and Railgun ran, before real money.

Risk

Audits so far are internal.

Answer

An external audit of circuits and contracts, then a capped launch.

Risk

A payer can take back a payment until the payee moves it.

Answer

Being fixed now: claiming a payment moves it to a note only the payee controls.

Risk

The crowd to hide in is small on testnet.

Answer

Payroll brings many payees at once, and agents bring volume.

Risk

A stablecoin issuer can freeze the pool contract.

Answer

Screen deposits so flagged funds never enter, so an issuer never needs to.

Risk

Privacy apps can fade, as Hinkal did.

Answer

Grow through payroll and payments that need privacy to work, not a pool people visit to hide.
14The 30-second answer

"What is Gloam standing on?"

Zama has FHE and Arcium has MPC. Both compute on encrypted data, and both need a committee to hold the key. Gloam stands on zero-knowledge proofs made on your own device. For payments that is the stronger foundation: every dollar has one owner, so nobody else ever needs a key, and a breach exposes one person instead of everyone. On top of it we built what finance actually asks for: proofs of funds, payment and payroll you can show to one person, for a set time. Private by default, provable on demand. Live on Tempo and Robinhood Chain testnets today.
